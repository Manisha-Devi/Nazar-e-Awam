# Verification methodology

**Route:** `/verification`

**Eyebrow:** How we work

Verification is a process of checking claims, records and context—not a label applied to every submission.

## From concern to publication

1. Receive the submission and record its stated scope.
2. Conduct an initial screen for public interest, clarity, safety and feasibility.
3. Review supporting documents without assuming that a submitted document is authentic.
4. Check relevant dates, records and context.
5. Seek independent corroboration where reasonably available.
6. Contact the concerned authority with specific factual questions.
7. Assess its response alongside contradictory evidence and unanswered questions.
8. Conduct editorial, privacy and legal-risk review.
9. Decide whether publication is justified by evidence and public interest.
10. Record significant later developments and corrections.

## Different kinds of information

- **Allegation:** a claim made by a person or source, not established merely by being submitted.
- **Documentary evidence:** material relevant to a claim; its origin, completeness and meaning still need assessment.
- **Independently corroborated information:** a claim supported by a separate source or record we have checked.
- **Official statement:** an account provided by an authority, attributed and assessed rather than treated as conclusive by default.
- **Editorial conclusion:** our reasoned assessment of the evidence and its limits.

Not every concern can be verified or published. We may ask for clarification; we do not promise coverage or a specific outcome.
